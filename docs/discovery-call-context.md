# Context from the discovery call

The call set out a fictional recruiting scenario. It supplies context; the [written assignment](../assignment/index.html) specifies the deliverables.

## The recruiting problem

A team of five recruiters sources people from LinkedIn, conferences, referrals and direct applications through Comeet. Each open role has one responsible recruiter. Comeet's built-in filtering is not accurate enough to help.

The core problem is helping a recruiter find and act on suitable people already in a scattered talent pool. Adding more candidates does not solve it.

## Pain points

- **Too much to screen.** Too much information and too little help deciding whom to approach first.
- **Lost conference contacts.** Each event's registration form feeds its own spreadsheet. Good hires have come from conferences, but past attendees are forgotten when a matching role opens later.
- **Hidden employee connections.** Referrals already get priority inside Comeet, but there is no equivalent for sourced people and conference attendees who have not applied. Colleagues who know them, or worked with them, could give a reference.

## What the solution needs to do

- **Treat attendance as neither qualification nor intent.** Some attendees are unsuitable; others are strong but not looking. LinkedIn URLs are optional on the form, so some profiles will be missing.
- **Make capture easy.** Registration should feed one shared pool directly instead of separate spreadsheets.
- **Fit the recruiter's day.** Roles open in Comeet. Show the relevant people for a role, explain each recommendation, and make the next step clear. Handle returning attendees, earlier conferences, people already in Comeet, and newly opened roles.
- **Reveal possible references without asking candidates.** Find who knows or worked with current employees from data already held, not by messaging attendees. A connection is a lead for a reference, not an endorsement.
- **Avoid another disconnected tool.** Building inside Comeet is not expected, but data should move between systems so recruiters manage each person in fewer places.
